Syntra minimax CBR-delay CCA · cc_sim · packet-level simulation

Search CCA in cc_sim

The Syntra CCA with a live depth-6 minimax search every round (no distilled rule): its exact QE belief over C, B and the queue, K hypotheses, change detection, and a planner that only picks the objective. Run over cc_sim's packet-level links on a focus set of ten cases, each a little past convergence, against Reno, Copa and the ack-window CCA (window = the acks of the last g min-RTTs, at least 4 packets). One control round is one handshake RTT (longer on slow links); the CCA picks its own units so that C stays inside the model's prior of 5 to 1000 units per round.

What the depth-6 runs show

  • Constant links and the step up: no loss after startup. No loss after warm-up on the static links (0.5, 1 and 4 BDP), at 1 Gbit/s, with acks at 3 ms gaps and after the step up. Startup costs 0.7–1.1% of the run on 0.5 and 1 BDP, the BDP-of-loss budget while C is uncertain. On the step up from 6 to 24 Mbit/s it does best of the five: utilization 0.87 at 1.4% loss, against 0.74 / 0.8% for Reno and 0.61 / 23.7% for the ack window with g=1.5.
  • Cruise sends below C. On 4 BDP Cruise picks DrainQueue in most rounds, at 38–45 units while min C is 48. The upper bound on the queue stays at 5–10 units, so the search keeps draining toward the target. Utilization after warm-up is 0.94 (0.98 on 1 BDP, where Cruise drained in all 23 Cruise rounds). This is the drain-and-refill tie-break seen earlier. Before changing the objective order, the next step is to read the root values with --decisions --exact-root.
  • Cellular loss comes from Recover, the one mode that is still a rule. TMobile loses 10.8% and ATT 17.4%. 94–99% of those losses are reported in Recover rounds: 21 and 18 belief restarts, and Recover takes 62% and 76% of the rounds. Recover's probe at about 1.5× delivery meets a falling rate, and the belief restarts again within 6–8 rounds. Loss is reported about two rounds after it is caused, but a Recover spell lasts six rounds, so most of this loss is caused there too.
  • Step down from 24 to 6 Mbit/s: 3.7% loss and utilization 0.82, against Reno at 2.8% / 0.94 and the ack window (g=1.5) at 0.36% / 0.91. There is one restart; most of the loss is reported in Probe rounds after the change.
  • 1 Gbit/s startup is slow: it takes 2.2 s to reach 90%, against 0.6 s for Reno and 0.8–0.9 s for the ack window. Startup grows about 1.5× per round. Steady state is full, with no loss and 20 ms of queue.
  • Poisson: 3.9% loss at utilization 0.92. Acks at 3 ms gaps: 0.09% loss at 0.90.

All scenarios

Whole run (the runs are short; startup is part of what is compared). Utilization is of the link's delivery opportunities. Restarts count the times the CCA's belief concluded the link changed; on a constant link every restart is a false one (the observations did not fit the model). Units: packets per model unit, and the round length, over the run.

Scenarios in detail

Reading the decisions

Every round the CCA reports what it sent, what the receiver acknowledged and what was lost, and the engine updates its belief: intervals for the link rate C, the buffer B and the queue, which provably contain the true values if the link behaves like the model (a constant-rate link that may serve up to a round late, with arrivals in bursts of up to K). The planner only picks a mode, which sets the objective and the candidate rates; a depth-6 minimax search (three rounds against the worst-case link) picks the rate. Every rate keeps the worst-case queue within one BDP; loss is budgeted over the last 20 rounds; the upper bound on C comes from the last 20 rounds only, so a rise in C gets looked for again. The text under the charts (and the hover) gives each round's reason.
